# AGENTS.md — World Rules (read before touching world code)

The city generator is a stack of hard invariants. **Every rule below was broken
at least once and had to be repaired.** If your change regresses one, it is a
bug — no matter how good the change looks otherwise. When you add a new rule,
add it here AND add an enforcement point (code guard or audit check).

## Definition of done — for ANY change to world generation or level crossings

1. `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx vite build` pass.
2. `npx tsx tools/audit-world.ts` reports all-PASS (run it with 2–3 different
   seed args: `npx tsx tools/audit-world.ts 4242`).
   For anything touching walkers, traffic or the player's footprint:
   `npx tsx tools/check-walkers.ts` (G8) and `npx tsx tools/check-traffic.ts` (G5) report PASS;
   for anything touching the getaway cars or how the traffic meets them, `npx tsx tools/check-traffic.ts --robbers` too (G7).
   For anything with visible text: `npx tsx tools/check-i18n.ts` reports PASS (G9).
   For anything touching the time of day or the sky: `npx tsx tools/check-daylight.ts` reports PASS (G10).
   For anything touching the sound effects or the music: `npx tsx tools/check-sfx.ts` reports PASS (G11, G12).
   For anything touching a mission scene: `npx tsx tools/check-scenes.ts` reports PASS (G4).
   For anything touching the graphics levels, the renderer or how far things are drawn: `npx tsx tools/check-quality.ts` reports PASS (G13).
   For anything touching the road vehicles' physics, bumps or resumes: `npx tsx tools/check-crash.ts` reports PASS (G1).
   For anything touching the calls (`missions.ts`) or how they follow the kid across islands: `npx tsx tools/check-missions.ts` reports PASS (G2).
   For anything touching the vehicles' steering or the street assist: `npx tsx tools/check-steering.ts` reports PASS (G19).
   For anything touching the wheel's input or its calibration page: `npx tsx tools/check-wheel.ts` reports PASS (G20).
   For anything touching auto speed or the arrival tests it stops for: `npx tsx tools/check-autospeed.ts` reports PASS (G18).
   For anything touching the race (its grid, the rivals, the rubber band): `npx tsx tools/check-race.ts` reports PASS (R32).
   For anything touching the airports or the plane's landings: `npx tsx tools/check-airport.ts` reports PASS (R41).
   For anything adding a ground layer, a deck, the foam or anything flat laid on the ground: `npx tsx tools/check-zfight.ts` reports PASS (R42) — run it with a few seeds.
   For anything touching the sea, its boats or what stands in the water: `npx tsx tools/check-sea.ts` reports PASS (G14).
   For anything touching the landmarks or the deliveries: `npx tsx tools/check-landmarks.ts` reports PASS (G16).
   For anything touching the stations, the kid's train or how it meets the level crossings: `npx tsx tools/check-trains.ts` and `npx tsx tools/check-traffic.ts --kid` report PASS (G6, R13).
3. Visual check in the browser for anything geometric (see *Verification*
   workflow below).
4. Scratch scripts in `.tmp/` are deleted; the fix is committed with a message
   that names the rule it preserves.

| R35 | **Road pieces never overlap, and they cover every street end to end.** No piece is laid on another (overlapping kerb bands cut across junctions and z-fight); pad reach + straights + pad reach = the edge's length on every street (the audit tests turned pieces by separating axes; an oblique pad as the fan of triangles about its node). ## Railway

| # | Rule | Enforced in |
|---|------|-------------|
| R7 | **The rail crosses streets square.** The railway is laid FIRST (against the causeway avenues and the river only) and the street generator lays every street around it: a street may cross a line only at 74° or better AND with the rail's worst heading on the carriageway within ±24 m of the crossing at 68° or better, at least 18 m from any junction — anything shallower is cut there (`railCut`/`railOk`). A street meeting a line askew jogs to cross it square (R37). Planned level-crossing streets cross each line dead square every ~125 m, and the ring road crosses each line on a spliced dead-square stretch. The rail's own deform (`perpendicularCrossings`) still squares it to the avenues it crosses. The bar is 25° worst in-corridor skew (measured at \|d\| < 6.5 m within ±24 m of the crossing). | `streetGen.ts railOk/railCut`, `railRoute.ts`, `audit-world.ts` |
| R8 | **The rail never lies on a road.** Parallel-with-clearance is fine, a square crossing is fine, overlap is not. Streets keep 12 m from the track unless crossing it square (the railside roads run 26 m out); the rail's `clearancePush()` keeps it 11.5 m off the causeway avenues it parallels (the portal stems run 24 m beside them); the plan still drops any street the rail would ride (`railRunsAlong`) as a guard. | `streetGen.ts`, `railRoute.ts`, `cityPlan.ts` |
| R8b | **A route ships only if its deformation provably worked.** `shapeQuality()` re-checks the finished path against the same numbers the audit uses (on-road rides, path folds, crossing skew) and `buildRoute()` deforms the top-scoring candidates in order, shipping the first clean one. The deform has rare bad modes on adversarial splines; a dirty result must cost a re-roll, never a shipped city. | `railRoute.ts buildRoute` |
| R9 | **Nothing built touches the track:** lots ≥16 m, street lamps ≥9 m from the rail centreline. | `cityPlan.ts addLot`, `cityChunk.ts` lamp guard |
| R10 | **A crossing never shares a square with a junction, and no crossing goes barrierless.** Every rail × street crossing is recorded (on the edge it crosses) at least 18 m from both of the edge's junctions — the generator refuses a node or an edge that would put one closer, and the plan drops any street that does (causeway avenues exempt: their crossings are recorded anyway — barriers beat a bare crossing). The audit fails any rail × street crossing without a recorded crossing. | `streetGen.ts railOk`, `cityPlan.ts nodeClash/crossings`, `audit-world.ts` |
| R11 | **Crossing arc distance `d` must come from cumulative arc length.** The deformed rail is NOT evenly spaced (crossing pins cram samples to ~0.2 m); a linear index→arc map misplaces `d` and mistimes every barrier. | `cityPlan.ts` `railCum` |
| R12 | **Every crossing has four posts** (two per road approach, 9.4 m up/down the street, one on each shoulder 7.6 m out), each with a crossbuck, flashing lamp pair and a boom. **Both face the approaching traffic**: the crossbuck's boards are rolled ±45° about their own facing axis and then turned so it runs along the street (Euler XYZ: `(0, heading, ±π/4)` — turned ±45° about the vertical they lay flat and the X faced the sky); the two lamps sit side by side ACROSS the road (along it they hid one behind the other) on a dark backplate on the crossing's side of the post. **Each boom yaws across the carriageway**: everything derives from the crossing's street `heading` (u = street direction, n = its normal); a post's local +z points back toward the centre line, so opposite shoulders always get opposite yaws — a boom pointing along the road blocks nothing. Arms grow along local +z; `pivot.rotation.x = -BOOM_UP` raises. They are HALF barriers: only the post on each approach's entry-lane shoulder carries a boom (driving +u the right-hand lane is n·side −1, driving −u it is +1), so the lane leaving the crossing stays open and a car caught inside can always drive out — booms on both lanes boxed it in. While the booms are down each approach carries a solid bar across its entry lane at the boom line (`Transit.boxesNear`, from just past the centre line to the kerb): nobody drives into a closed crossing (hitting it is a G1 bump). | `transit.ts makeCrossing` |
| R13 | **Booms + lamps warn when a train within `CROSSING_WARN_DIST` (60 m) will reach the crossing within `CROSSING_LEAD` (10 s), until its tail is 2 m past** — one rule, `Railway.crossingWarns`, that the booms, the car AI and the walkers (at 90 m and 14 s: they are slow) all ask, so they stay in sync. A train standing at a platform or a signal short of the crossing doesn't hold it down, nor one going away: counting any train within 60 m either side kept a crossing by a station down 59 s a minute and gridlocked the streets round it (seed 144795619, island (3,2)). A car stops with its nose 1.2 m short of the boom line (`CROSSING_BOOM` = 9.4 m, shared with the posts); a car already past that point when the warning starts, or caught inside by a queue, never stays on the crossing — it drives on until its tail clears the far boom, a red beyond notwithstanding; and it only starts over a crossing if wherever it may have to stop beyond it (behind the car ahead, or at the junction ahead with everybody beyond the crossing queued there — a red, a green that runs out before it reaches the stop line: `greenLeft`, or a give-way or roundabout while somebody is on it or queued for it) leaves its whole length past the far boom — a give-way counted always left no room past a crossing 12 m short of it, and cars waited there for good (seed 144795619, island (1,0)). No car spawns on a crossing, and no car turns into a street whose level crossing (within 45 m past the curve) is warning — once round the curve it couldn't stop short of the boom and drove on in front of the train: it waits short of the junction. **The kid's train** (not on the timetable) is measured from its real nose and tail (its pose point is its first car's middle, 4.5 m behind the nose) and predicted like a timetable train: the warning is on while it could reach the crossing within `lead` s pulling away flat out from its present speed; standing still with no mind to go, or with its doors open (G6), it holds nothing down. Pulling away from a standstill with a crossing within 60 m ahead it waits 2 s first — the lamps, the horn, the booms coming down — once per standstill (pressing the gas again and again restarted the wait each time and the train never set off: `check-trains` pumps the pedal); and it brakes and waits (a toot every 4 s, `train.waitCrossing`) for anybody on a crossing within its stopping distance + 12 m (`Railway.obstacleAt`, the islands' cars and walkers): before, the booms only started down as it moved, and cars drove out in front of it. `tools/check-traffic.ts --kid` drives it among two islands' traffic and fails if anybody is on a crossing as its nose reaches it. | `railway.ts crossingWarns`, `transit.ts`, `island/cars.ts`, `island/walkers.ts` |
| R14 | **Each line is a deformed OPEN polyline (`polyPath`), not a spline, laid gently (1–2 free control points, 20–70 m wander) and kept off the river's side.** Every deform pass is open-path (no wrap-around indexing; the end vertices stay). The stems and the diamond are pinned before the deform — so every crossing pin squares against the final stems — and re-snapped after it with a short fade (a long post-deform fade dragged square crossings 38° askew). Never rebuild it through `makePath`/a smoother — re-smoothing undoes the perpendicular crossings. Sharp corners get normalized by `roundCorners()` (apex replaced by two points ~32% down each leg, then a clearance push so a cut can't skim asphalt); `deSpikes()` repairs reversals and spliced U-turns on a re-densified copy. | `railRoute.ts finalize/deform` |
| R15 | **Kenney templates have non-native origins/sizes.** The railroad tile sits ~1 m below its own origin (measure minY and lift — `bakeRails`); road kit tiles are 1 m native. The Toy Car Kit models its vehicles facing −z (front wheels at −z) where the Car Kit and the game face +z: its race cars carry `yaw: π` in `raceCars.ts` (the Car Kit's F1, future racer and karts carry 0) — probe a vehicle kit's wheel nodes before using it. `bakeModel`'s `s3` is a raw scale vector applied *before* rotation. Always probe native bounds before placing a template. | `railRoute.ts`, `assets.ts` |
| R16 | **Trains are world-level and run on a timetable** (`railway.ts`). Every line runs one way (seeded per column / row) with a train every `HEADWAY` = 60 s entering each portal at the line's own phase; each island's segment is timed to a whole number of headways (cruise tuned ±25%, the rest absorbed as platform dwell ≤36 s or a signal halt), so a train leaves at the phase it entered and the next island picks it up seamlessly. Position is a pure function of the game clock: `Railway.distTo(bx, by, line, arc, t)` answers crossing queries (and the cars' catch-up after a dormant spell) for any time. Nothing leaves a waiting train across a level crossing: a signal halt is placed clear of every crossing, and a station keeps `STATION_CLEAR` (36 m) along its line behind its stop board from every crossing and `STATION_AHEAD` (14 m) past it (`cityPlan.ts` stage 7: a passenger train, three cars ~30 m, standing with its head at the board — a line runs one way, `railRoute.ts lineDir`, shared by the plan and the timetable — and 6 m more; **freight trains don't stop at stations**, they carry nobody) — stations 24 m off let trains stand on a crossing through every dwell (seed 7, island (1,0)), and 56 m either way round left an island's line one station or none; stations stand as many as fit `STATION_GAP` (250 m) apart, about 4 an island's line, a stop every 250–400 m; the audit fails any crossing a timetable train covers for over 25 s (R16). Only trains within 320 m of the player are drawn; a view is reused by the nearest train of the same line, so crossing a portal never pops. | `railway.ts`, `transit.ts`, `island/cars.ts`, `cityPlan.ts STATION_CLEAR`, `audit-world.ts` (R16) |

## The occupancy grid

| # | Rule | Enforced in |
|---|------|-------------|
| R22 | **Never rail over river over road.** Where the rail sits in the water, no road bridge may sit within 24 m — a trestle sharing the water with a bridge is the one rail/river/road pileup the world forbids. Enforced three ways: the street generator never lays a river crossing within 30 m of a trestle (the rail is laid first), `shapeQuality` counts trestle-near-bridge runs against the causeway avenues' bridges and the quality gate re-rolls tainted candidates, and `cityPlan` drops any street whose bridge would clash with a trestle span — the dead-end trim repairs the web. | `railRoute.ts`, `cityPlan.ts bridgeClash` |
| R23 | **The occupancy grid is the shared authority on what occupies where.** `occupancyFor(bx,by)` paints river, streets (true bands at any angle, plus oblique pads), plazas, rail and lots (turned footprints) into one 1 m bitmask per city (ROAD=1, RAIL=2, RIVER=4, LOT=8, PLAZA=16, SEA=32, DECK=128; overlaps OR together — ROAD\|RAIL is a crossing, ROAD\|RIVER a bridge, RAIL\|RIVER a trestle). EVERY prop/lamp/traffic-light/pedestrian placement asks the grid (`claims`) instead of hand-rolling distance checks against individual generators — `nearStreet` is gone. Forbidden combinations (LOT over street/track/water/plaza, and R22's triple) must count zero; `tools/audit-world.ts` scans the grid every run. Lots are oriented rectangles turned to their street: footprints (not just centres) must clear the river, streets, pads and the track, and no two lots may overlap — `addLot` checks the outline and a separating-axis overlap (lots keep `LOT_GAP` = 0.8 m apart); collision boxes of turned footprints carry an OBB (`inBox`). Tree rows check LOT with an own-lot exemption so a wandering tree never lands in a neighbouring building. | `grid.ts`, `cityChunk.ts`, `lampProps.ts`, `pedestrians.ts`, `cityPlan.ts addLot`, `audit-world.ts` |
| R24 | **Each railway line is continuous welded track — tiles must connect, and a line runs portal to portal.** `bakeRails` emits each piece as the exact chord between shared end vertices (mitred corner-to-corner, never oriented by a stale run heading), subdividing the deformed polyline's long segments first and capping how far a piece bows off the path. `deform` repairs hairpins (`deSpikes`): exact reversals lose their apex; a >120° U-turn smeared over three short segments has its two middle vertices spliced out, and whenever anything was spliced the crossing tail re-runs on a re-densified (≤2 m) copy — pins cannot grip a 30 m chord straddling a street. No fallback may ship a folded route; the audit fails reversals and windowed hairpins alike. | `railRoute.ts bakeRails/deSpikes/deform`, `audit-world.ts` |
| R26′ | **The river obeys the same geometry laws as the streets.** It lives in one north-south lane, keeping out of the lanes either side of the north / south causeway avenues and out of the west / east rail stems, reaches the ocean at both ends (shore to shore), and never runs beneath a street: a street crosses it only within 25° of square (35° for the ring and avenues, 10° for late links), no two crossings within 45 m of each other and none within 30 m of a trestle; it is then straightened square to every crossing AS BUILT (`straightenAt`, two passes, ±26 m windows) so each bridge meets the water at a right angle (audit: ≤12°). The riverside embankment roads run 34 m out, with square bridges every ~170 m. | `riverRoute.ts`, `streetGen.ts riverCut`, `audit-world.ts` (R26) |
| R27 | **Trestles meet the water at a right angle too.** `riverPerp()` pins every in-water stretch onto the river's normal through the stretch midpoint (weight 1 in the water, cosine fade outside) and runs with the LAST word in `deform` — street swings upstream may not leave a skewed trestle. The scorer penalizes skewed water crossings, `shapeQuality` counts worst in-water skew (accept <30°), and the audit fails shipped routes over 30°. | `railRoute.ts riverPerp/shapeQuality/tryRoute`, `audit-world.ts` |
| R28 | **A line never overlaps itself.** No two non-adjacent stretches of a line come within 3.6 m (the 3.4 m bed width) — needle folds are spliced out by the de-overlapper in the deform pipeline; on the timetable consecutive trains of a line stay ≥60 m apart (the audit samples every island). | `railRoute.ts` deOverlap, `railway.ts`, audit R28/R31 |
| R30 | **The portal contract: two through lines per island that weld to the neighbours'.** The north-south line starts on the north rim at x = `southExit(bx, by−1)`·64 + 24 and ends on the southern neighbour's rim (z = `CITY_PITCH`) at x = `southExit(bx, by)`·64 + 24; the west-east line likewise on the east exits. Both run dead straight for the 180 m `STEM` inland from each rim and across the strait (the strait south / east of an island belongs to that island's lines), 24 m beside the causeway avenue, so neighbouring lines meet end to end. The river keeps out of the two lanes the north-south stems use. The timetable is continuous across portals too: no train head ever jumps. | `railRoute.ts railPortals/pinFrame`, `riverRoute.ts`, `railway.ts`, `audit-world.ts` (R30) |
| R31 | **One square diamond per island, and never two trains on it.** The lines cross exactly once, at the centre of a block (32 m from every street, ≥44 m from the river, ≥140 m inland), both pinned dead straight ±16 m through it at right angles; a second crossing is a rejected candidate. Transit lays one diamond plate carrying all four rails. On the timetable the west-east train's diamond window (head 3 m short … tail 3 m past) never overlaps a north-south one's modulo the headway — it holds at the platform before the diamond or at a signal short of it; the kid's train (north-south) gets a red while a west-east train is due and can't stop on the diamond. | `railRoute.ts pickDiamond/pinFrame`, `railway.ts segment/driveKid`, `transit.ts`, `audit-world.ts` (R31) |
| R25 | **Neighbouring seeds grow visibly different cities.** `chunkSeed` finishes with a full murmur3 avalanche — multiplication alone keeps low bits linear in the seed, so seeds 7 and 8 ran partially-correlated streams. The macro layout is seeded too, not fixed: per-city street-line density (keep 0.38–0.72), per-corner nature biomes (occasionally city), and an oval-vs-round rail ring of varying radius. The audit compares seeds S/S+1 and S+1/S+2 on street lines, segments, district map and rail shape (R25 thresholds) on islands (1,0) and (1,1), and the race circuit of island (0,0), and fails a pair that reads alike. | `engine/rng.ts`, `cityGrid.ts`, `cityPlan.ts`, `railRoute.ts`, `audit-world.ts` |

## World structure

| # | Rule | Enforced in |
|---|------|-------------|

## Verification workflow

- **Audit (numbers beat vibes):** `npx tsx tools/audit-world.ts <seed>` checks
  R1, R7 (worst in-corridor skew ≤25°: the rail's worst angle to the street
  while on its asphalt, |d| < 6.5 m, ±24 m along), R8 (no near-parallel rail
  run <7 m from a road centreline for ≥8 m), R9, R10 indirectly (crossing
  count > 0), R19, R24 (zero hairpin folds on any shipped route), R25
  (seeds S/S+1/S+2 must grow visibly different cities), R26 (river inside
  its lane, shore to shore, perpendicular street crossings), R27 (zero
  trestle skew), R28 (a line never overlaps itself, checked across a
  9×9-city ring), R30 (portals weld; stems straight; no timetable train
  ever jumps), R31 (one square diamond; never two trains on it; no
  tailgating), R38 (a worker-built island installs identical to a local build), R32 (race islands: a closed kit loop in its own block, the apron clear, four mouths), R33 (built blocks ≥55% built over; districts ring out from the centre, industry by the
  track), R34 (arms ≥55°, edges ≥32 m), R37 (no block over 150 000 m²), R35 (road pieces never overlap and cover every street),
  and — via the occupancy grid — R22/R23/R29 (zero forbidden combination
  cells, nothing built in the sea but causeway decks; the boat lane clears
  every deck).
  Road looks: `dev-roads.html?seed=N` bakes real chunks and lists one node
  of every pad kind (cross, tee, bend, pass, end, round, pad-N, with -lights,
  roundabout, end) — frame one with `&x=..&z=..&h=45&tilt=35`. Extend this tool whenever you add a rule — one rule,
  one check. Zero tolerance: the deform modes are seed-dependent, so sweep
  several seeds
  (`for s in 7 777 4242 2024 9999 3 21 5; do npx tsx tools/audit-world.ts $s; done`)
  — a clean default seed proves nothing about the seed the player rolls.
- **Gates:** `npm run typecheck` (src + tools) `&& npx vite build`.
- **Refactors that must not change the world:** `npx tsx tools/plan-hash.ts`
  before and after (13 cities × 8 seeds; `--dump dir` writes the canonical
  text for diffing). Different hashes = the refactor changed a city.
- **Visual:** dev server runs on port 8321 (reuse the running one). Load
  `play/city.html?seed=N&debugsea=1` — `window.__dbg` exposes
  scene/camera/renderer/player/railway/trains()/traffic/transit/chunks/missions/route()
  (`trains()` = the current island's two timetables; `route()` = its rail net).
  Automation tabs are usually hidden, which pauses requestAnimationFrame and
  throttles timers to 1 s: step the game with `__dbg.tick()` in a loop that
  busy-waits ~16 ms per frame (so `dt` is real), and drive input with
  `dispatchEvent(new KeyboardEvent('keydown', { code: 'KeyW' }))`. Take
  screenshots via canvas readback
  (`renderer.render(...); renderer.domElement.toDataURL('image/png')`) — the
  IAB `tab.screenshot()` works only once per tab. Teleport by assigning
  `__dbg.player.state.x/z`, then wait ~2 s for chunk streaming. The live loop
  owns the camera in chase modes; pose it inside the same `evaluate` that
  renders.
- **Find things fast:** signalized junctions = `__dbg.chunks.chunks` entries
  with `.lights` (post world positions are the *children*'s positions, not the
  group's); crossings = `__dbg.transit.list()`; rail geometry =
  `__dbg.route().lines[0|1].pts`, the diamond `__dbg.route().diamond`.
  Train models load asynchronously — yield (`await` a timeout) before a
  screenshot, or a tight tick loop renders the track without consists.
